Understanding Pest Control

A pest infestation is more than a nuisance. Left unchecked, pests like termites and rodents can cause thousands of dollars in structural damage, while roaches and bed bugs can trigger allergies and disrupt sleep. The good news is that modern pest control is far more targeted, family-friendly, and pet-safe than treatments from even a decade ago.

Common household pests

In the U.S., the most frequently treated pests fall into a handful of categories:

  • Roaches — German, American, and Oriental species thrive in kitchens and bathrooms
  • Ants — odorous house ants, carpenter ants, and fire ants are among the top offenders
  • Rodents — house mice, Norway rats, and roof rats are common in attics, garages, and basements
  • Termites — subterranean and drywood termites cause an estimated $5 billion in U.S. damage each year
  • Bed bugs — fast-spreading hitchhikers found in bedrooms, hotels, and used furniture
  • Mosquitoes, fleas, ticks, and spiders — seasonal threats with serious health implications

Treatment options

Modern pest control uses Integrated Pest Management (IPM), which combines targeted chemical treatments, baits, traps, exclusion (sealing entry points), and habitat modification. IPM is designed to be effective while minimizing pesticide exposure for kids and pets.

How much does it cost?

A one-time treatment typically runs $150 to $400 depending on pest type and home size. Quarterly maintenance plans average $40 to $70 per visit and include warranty coverage between treatments. Termite and bed bug jobs are usually quoted separately and can range from $500 to several thousand dollars.

When to call a pro

  • You have seen droppings, gnaw marks, or live pests
  • DIY treatments are not solving the problem
  • You suspect termites (mud tubes, hollow wood, swarmers)
  • Bed bug bites or stains on your mattress
  • You want preventative seasonal treatment
