Why Connecticut Homeowners Need a Local Pest Pro

Connecticut has some of the highest home values and oldest housing stock in the country — much of it pre-1940 wood frame and colonial construction that creates significant termite and carpenter ant pressure. Hartford made the biggest leap in Orkin's 2025 Top 50 Bed Bug Cities list, jumping 48 spots to debut at #35 — the fastest single-year rise nationally. Connecticut's dense suburban development adjacent to wooded areas also creates significant tick pressure that many homeowners address through pest control programs.

All commercial pest control companies in Connecticut must be licensed by the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection (DEEP). Pesticide applicators must hold a Commercial Pesticide Applicator Certificate in the appropriate category. Verify licensing at ct.gov/deep before hiring any exterminator, and ask for proof of liability insurance plus a written treatment plan with a re-service guarantee.

Connecticut Pest Control FAQs

How much does pest control cost in Connecticut?

Connecticut pest control costs are above the national average due to higher labor costs and cost of living. One-time treatments typically run $150-425. Fairfield County and the I-95 corridor markets (Stamford, Greenwich, Norwalk) trend toward the higher end. Bed bug heat treatment averages $950-2,600. Termite treatment runs $550-2,400 for a typical Connecticut home.

Does Connecticut require pest control licensing?

Yes. Connecticut requires all commercial pest control companies to be licensed by the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection (DEEP). Pesticide applicators must hold a Commercial Pesticide Applicator Certificate in the appropriate category. Verify licensing at ct.gov/deep before hiring any exterminator.

Why did Hartford jump 48 spots in bed bug rankings?

Hartford debuted at #35 on Orkin's 2025 Top 50 Bed Bug Cities list after jumping an unprecedented 48 spots — the largest single-year rise in the entire national ranking. This rapid increase indicates a significant surge in bed bug activity in the Hartford metro. Contributing factors likely include the city's older rental housing stock, high student population near Trinity College and University of Hartford, and increased travel through Bradley International Airport. Early professional intervention is strongly recommended for Hartford area homeowners who suspect bed bug activity.
