About Florida pest control

Florida is the most pest-active state in the continental United States — no other state has Florida's combination of year-round heat, extreme humidity, diverse pest species, and massive human population density. Florida has no winter pest dormancy whatsoever. Every major pest type — termites, cockroaches, mosquitoes, fire ants, rodents, and bed bugs — remains fully active every month of the year.

Florida leads the nation in termite diversity with three separate termite species active in the state including the highly aggressive Formosan termite in South Florida and the Gulf Coast and the Asian subterranean termite found almost nowhere else in the continental US. The state's tourism industry — over 130 million visitors annually — creates bed bug introduction pressure unlike any other state in the country. Statewide pest control is a year-round necessity rather than a seasonal service.

Florida Pest Control FAQs

How much does pest control cost in Florida?

Florida pest control costs $75-250 for a standard one-time treatment — generally higher than northern states due to the year-round pest pressure requiring more intensive treatment protocols. Termite treatment ranges $500-3,000 — Formosan termite treatment in South Florida tends to run higher. Mosquito control programs run $60-100 per monthly treatment. Bed bug treatment runs $300-1,500. Getting multiple quotes is strongly recommended.

Does Florida require pest control licensing?

Yes. All structural pest control companies in Florida must be licensed by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services Bureau of Entomology and Pest Control. Florida has some of the most comprehensive pest control licensing requirements in the country. Verify any company's license at freshfromflorida.com before hiring. Always confirm licensing and liability insurance before allowing treatment on your property.

What makes Florida pest problems unique compared to other states?

Florida is the only continental US state with three active termite species — Eastern subterranean, Formosan, and Asian subterranean — all simultaneously active year-round. No other state has this termite diversity or intensity. Florida's palmetto bugs — American cockroaches that grow to two inches — are larger and more resilient than cockroaches in northern markets. The state's tourism dominance creates bed bug introduction pressure at a scale no other state matches. Florida homeowners must think about pest control as a year-round necessity rather than a seasonal service — there is no off-season.
