About Washington pest control

Washington State's pest landscape divides sharply along the Cascade Mountains. Western Washington — Seattle, Tacoma, Vancouver, and the Puget Sound corridor — faces persistent moisture-driven pest pressure year-round. The wet climate that makes the region lush and green also creates ideal conditions for carpenter ants, odorous house ants, subterranean termites, and rodents that remain active through the mild Pacific Northwest winters.

Eastern Washington — Spokane, the Tri-Cities, and the Yakima Valley — has a drier high desert climate more similar to Idaho, with different ant species, field mice, and summer wasp pressure replacing the moisture-driven pests of the west side. Washington is also the only US state outside the Pacific Northwest where dampwood termites are a common concern, alongside standard Eastern subterranean termites in older wood-frame construction.

Washington Pest Control FAQs

How much does pest control cost in Washington State?

Washington pest control costs $75-225 for a standard one-time treatment. Carpenter ant treatment — one of the most common services in western Washington — runs $150-400 depending on the extent of activity. Termite treatment ranges $500-2,500. Rodent exclusion work runs $200-600. Washington's competitive pest control market means pricing varies — getting multiple quotes is recommended.

Does Washington require pest control licensing?

Yes. All structural pest control companies in Washington must be licensed by the Washington State Department of Agriculture Pesticide Management Division. Verify any company's license at agr.wa.gov/pesticides before hiring. Always confirm licensing and liability insurance before allowing treatment.

What makes Washington pest control different from other states?

Western Washington's wet climate creates pest conditions unlike most of the country — dampwood termites found nowhere else in the continental US are present in the Pacific Northwest alongside standard subterranean termites. Carpenter ants are significantly more prevalent in Washington than in drier states because the moisture keeps wood soft and workable for colonies. Odorous house ants — so named for the coconut-like smell when crushed — are the most common pest complaint in western Washington and are notoriously difficult to eliminate without professional treatment. Eastern Washington's drier climate means completely different pest profiles within the same state.
