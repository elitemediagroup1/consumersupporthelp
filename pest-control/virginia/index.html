About Virginia pest control

Virginia's pest landscape spans three distinct zones — Northern Virginia near Washington DC with urban pest dynamics, the Piedmont with termite and tick pressure, and coastal Tidewater with year-round mosquito and termite activity from the Chesapeake Bay and its tributaries. Eastern subterranean termites are active throughout Virginia with heavy activity in the Tidewater region.

The Brown Marmorated Stink Bug — a significant Virginia pest — invades homes by the thousands each fall seeking warmth. Virginia's military presence across Hampton Roads creates unique bed bug introduction pressure through housing turnover. Tidewater Virginia's Chesapeake Bay and river system creates some of the most significant mosquito habitat on the East Coast, while the historic crawl space construction in Richmond, Norfolk, and Virginia Beach faces elevated termite vulnerability.

Virginia Pest Control FAQs

How much does pest control cost in Virginia?

Virginia pest control costs $75-215 for a standard one-time treatment. Termite treatment ranges $500-2,500 depending on method and home size. Mosquito control programs run $50-90 per monthly treatment. Stink bug exclusion work runs $200-500 depending on the extent of entry points. Getting multiple quotes is recommended.

Does Virginia require pest control licensing?

Yes. All structural pest control companies in Virginia must be licensed by the Virginia Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services Office of Pesticide Services. Verify any company's license at vdacs.virginia.gov/pesticides before hiring. Always confirm licensing and liability insurance before allowing treatment.

What makes Virginia pest problems unique?

Virginia's most distinctive pest is the Brown Marmorated Stink Bug — an invasive species that arrived in the US through the mid-Atlantic region and reaches nuisance levels in Virginia each fall as populations seek warmth inside homes. Virginia's military concentration in Hampton Roads — the largest naval installation in the world at Naval Station Norfolk — creates ongoing bed bug introduction pressure through military housing turnover unlike most states. Tidewater Virginia's Chesapeake Bay and river system creates some of the most significant mosquito habitat on the East Coast.
