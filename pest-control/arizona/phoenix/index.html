Why Phoenix homeowners deal with scorpions and termites differently than most of the country

Phoenix sits in the native range of the bark scorpion — the only medically significant scorpion in the United States. Bark scorpions can squeeze through gaps as small as a credit card and climb almost any surface, which is why barrier treatment and exclusion are both essential for Phoenix homes. The Phoenix metro also ranks in the top 10 worst termite cities nationally. Unlike northern states where termite season is limited to a few months, Arizona's warm winters allow termite activity nearly year-round.

New construction across the Phoenix metro frequently disturbs established scorpion and termite colonies in surrounding desert soil, pushing them into adjacent neighborhoods. Monsoon season (July through September) brings a second peak — heavy rains drive scorpions, cockroaches, and roof rats into homes seeking higher ground, and termite swarmers emerge after significant rain events. Quarterly prevention plans are the most cost-effective approach for most Phoenix homeowners.

Phoenix Pest Control FAQs

How much does pest control cost in Phoenix?

Phoenix pest control costs $75-200 for a standard one-time treatment. Scorpion barrier treatment — the most requested service in the Phoenix metro — runs $100-250 for an initial treatment with quarterly follow-ups at $75-150. Termite treatment costs $500-2,500 depending on the method. Phoenix's year-round warm climate means pest pressure never fully stops, making quarterly prevention plans the most popular and cost-effective choice for homeowners.

Why are scorpions such a problem in Phoenix?

Phoenix sits in the native range of the bark scorpion — the only medically significant scorpion species in the United States. Bark scorpions are excellent climbers and can squeeze through gaps as small as a credit card, making them particularly difficult to keep out of homes. New construction in the Phoenix metro frequently disturbs established scorpion populations, pushing them into adjacent neighborhoods. Professional barrier treatment applied quarterly is the most effective control method.

Does Arizona require pest control licensing in Phoenix?

Yes. All pest control companies operating in Phoenix and Maricopa County must be licensed by the Arizona Office of Pest Management (OPM). Verify any company's license at azda.gov/opm before hiring. Maricopa County has significant pest pressure including bark scorpions, termites, and roof rats — working with a licensed professional is both legally required and essential for effective treatment.

How bad are termites in Phoenix?

Phoenix ranks among the top 10 worst termite cities nationally. Unlike northern states where termite season is limited to spring and summer, Arizona's warm winters allow termite activity nearly year-round. The Phoenix metro has both subterranean termites — which travel through soil — and drywood termites, which infest wood directly. New construction in suburban Phoenix frequently disturbs termite colonies in surrounding desert soil. Annual termite inspections are strongly recommended for all Phoenix homeowners.

What time of year is worst for pests in Phoenix?

Phoenix pest pressure is year-round but peaks during two distinct windows. Spring (March-May) brings termite swarm season and the beginning of scorpion activity as temperatures warm. Monsoon season (July-September) is Phoenix's second peak — heavy rains drive scorpions, cockroaches, and roof rats into homes seeking higher ground, and termite swarmers emerge after monsoon rains. Fall and winter see reduced but not eliminated pest activity — scorpions remain active until temperatures consistently drop below 60 degrees.
