California Home Security FAQ

Does California have specific laws about security cameras?

Yes. California has strict privacy laws including the California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA) that affect camera placement. Cameras must not be positioned to intentionally capture images of neighboring properties or areas where people have a reasonable expectation of privacy. California also has laws regarding recording of conversations. Licensed installers familiar with California requirements can ensure your system is positioned compliantly. When in doubt position cameras to cover your property only.

What home security systems work best for California renters?

California's coastal cities have among the highest renter concentrations in the country. Wireless no-drill systems dominate the California renter market — adhesive-mounted sensors, video doorbells, and indoor cameras install without permanent modifications and are fully portable. California law provides strong tenant protections and in most cases renters have the right to install security devices that do not permanently alter the property. Review your lease for specific restrictions.

Monthly monitoring runs $20–$60 regardless of location. Bay Area installation may cost $200–$400+. Los Angeles installation typically runs $150–$300. Does home security reduce insurance costs in California?

California homeowners insurance is among the most expensive in the country particularly in high-fire-risk areas. Most carriers offer 5–15% discounts for monitored security systems. Some carriers also offer additional discounts for smoke and CO sensors, water leak detection, and other smart home safety devices. Ask your insurer about the full range of available discounts for your California home.

What is porch piracy and how do I prevent it in California?

Porch piracy — theft of packages from unattended doorsteps — is a significant concern throughout California. California's warm climate and high e-commerce adoption mean packages are frequently left at unattended doors. Video doorbells that capture footage of delivery areas, cameras covering porch and driveway areas, and smart locks that allow in-door delivery for certain carriers are the most effective deterrents. Many California homeowners also use package lockboxes in combination with camera coverage.
