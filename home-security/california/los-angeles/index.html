Los Angeles Home Security FAQ

Does the City of Los Angeles require alarm registration?

Yes. The City of Los Angeles requires alarm system permits and registration with the LAPD. Unregistered alarms can result in fines and may affect police response priority. Los Angeles uses a verified response policy for alarm calls — requiring video or audio confirmation before dispatching officers in many cases. Licensed installers handle permit and registration as part of installation. Ask your installer to confirm they will complete LAPD registration.

What security options work for LA apartment renters?

Los Angeles's massive renter market is primarily served by wireless no-drill systems. Adhesive-mounted door and window sensors, indoor cameras, and video doorbells compatible with building intercom systems are the most popular options. Older Silver Lake, Echo Park, and Hollywood apartment buildings may have older intercom systems that require specialist knowledge to integrate. Review your lease for any restrictions and notify your landlord if required.

Is package theft a big problem in LA?

Yes. Package theft is one of the most frequently reported property crimes in Los Angeles. The city's high e-commerce adoption rate, warm year-round climate that keeps deliveries frequent, and large number of multi-unit buildings where packages are left in unsecured lobbies all contribute. Video doorbells, porch cameras, and package lockboxes are the most effective deterrents. Many LA residents use both camera coverage and package-specific security measures.

How much does home security cost in Los Angeles?

Los Angeles is above the national average for home security costs due to higher labor rates and the competitive but expensive California market. Equipment runs $150–$1,500+ depending on system size. Professional installation typically runs $150–$350. Monthly monitoring runs $20–$60. Multiple providers compete in the LA market. Call for specific quotes for your address and system needs.

Do LA homeowners need to worry about camera privacy laws?

Yes. California has strict privacy laws around camera placement. In Los Angeles cameras must be positioned to cover your own property only — not neighboring yards, windows, or areas where people have a reasonable expectation of privacy. Licensed installers familiar with California requirements ensure compliant positioning. Cameras covering public sidewalks from your property are generally permitted but should be disclosed to neighbors as a courtesy.

What is the best security setup for an older LA neighborhood home?

Older LA neighborhoods like Silver Lake, Echo Park, Highland Park, and Pasadena have early 20th century construction with multiple entry points. A standard setup includes door sensors on all entry doors, window sensors on accessible windows, at least one interior motion detector, a video doorbell or front camera, outdoor cameras covering driveway and rear yard, and professional monitoring. LA's year-round activity makes outdoor camera coverage especially valuable compared to seasonal markets.

What security options exist for LA short-term rental hosts?

Los Angeles has a significant Airbnb and vacation rental market throughout the city and surrounding beach communities. Smart locks with guest-specific access codes that expire after checkout are the most popular feature for LA short-term rental hosts. Indoor cameras are NOT permitted in guest spaces under Airbnb policy and California privacy law. Outdoor cameras covering entry points and driveways are appropriate. Noise monitoring devices are popular for LA hosts concerned about parties.

Is home security worth it in safer LA neighborhoods like Brentwood?

Even in safer LA neighborhoods like Brentwood, Pacific Palisades, and San Marino — where burglary rates are low — security systems provide meaningful value. Package theft affects virtually every LA neighborhood regardless of income level. Remote monitoring for frequent travelers is valuable. Smart home integration and the deterrent effect of visible cameras are additional benefits. Many affluent LA homeowners install robust systems for remote monitoring and smart home control rather than crime deterrence.
